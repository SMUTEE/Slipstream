Launch plan · Slipstream · October 2026

Pass The Loop

Slipstream already contains the only growth mechanic it needs: a link that carries the music, and that the person opening it can change. Everything below exists to get that link into circulation. Impressions are not the goal, and optimising for them will produce worse content.

It is live

slipstream-smute.vercel.app is public and serving. Vercel deploys it on every push, modules load, audio runs at 5 ms, and a share link round-trips through a real address. The phone layout fits one screen with the record button pinned.

Use the root URL in posts, because the landing page gives a proper link preview and is one tap from Play. Use /app/ for bio links, where saving a tap matters more than a preview.

Three things left before you post, none of them large:

1. Open it on your own iPhone and press a pad. Everything so far was Chrome with touch emulation. If the first note is late or silent on iOS Safari, you want to know before you send traffic, not after.
2. Turn on Vercel Analytics (project, Analytics, Enable). Free, and it gives you the referrer breakdown that tells you which platform actually sent people.
3. Make three seed loops and save their codes. You cannot ask people to pass a loop if you have not passed one first.

Strategy

What this campaign is actually optimising for

A browser chord tool is a niche product. Chasing a ten million view moment will push you toward content that gets watched and forgotten. The compounding thing is different.

THE REAL METRICLinks opened, not views Someone opening a shared arrangement has heard your product, in their browser, with no install. That is a converted user. A view is not.
THE MECHANICThe relay Post four bars. Someone adds a layer and posts theirs. The share token makes this native, so the format is not a gimmick bolted on, it is the product working.
THE HOOK"I don't know what that chord is" The product's actual promise. Lead with the feeling of getting away with something, not with the feature list.
THE MOATThe rhythm engine, not yet built A 12-pulse Afrobeats engine on the roadmap is the reason to follow rather than just try. Tease it honestly as coming, never as shipped.
Honest ceiling. Realistic good outcome in three weeks: one short-form video between 50k and 500k if the audio hook lands, a few hundred to low thousands of X engagements among music-tech and indie devs, and a LinkedIn post that does well inside product and design circles. What would make this a real launch is a hundred people passing a loop, and that number is far more achievable and worth far more than a million passive views.

The one technical thing to add first. Switch on Vercel Analytics. It is free and gives you visits by referrer, which is how you tell TikTok from X. It will not tell you how many people opened a shared arrangement, because the token lives in the URL fragment and a fragment never reaches a server. Count those from tags and replies instead, by hand. It is the number that matters most and the only one you have to gather yourself.

XTechnical thread7 posts 
1/ some notes on building it. no framework, no dependencies, no build step. nineteen es modules and the web audio api.

2/ the seven pads are scale degrees. the engine stores "degree 5 with a 7th", never "G7". change key and every pad transposes. change scale and the qualities reshape. one data structure, zero special cases.

3/ the 7th is diatonic. one knob position gives you Cmaj7 on the tonic, Dm7 on the supertonic, Bm7♭5 on the leading tone. the chord you'd actually want, without a lookup table.

4/ slide is real portamento. a fixed five voice pool whose oscillators never stop, so a chord change ramps frequency instead of retriggering. hold a chord, change key, hear it glide.

5/ loops store beats, not seconds. the loop is bars × 4 beats and tempo maps to real time only at playback. move the bpm and the whole arrangement moves together. i got this wrong first and it desynced everything.

6/ the whole arrangement packs into a url token. key, scale, tempo, every layer. a four chord loop is 38 characters. and because layers hold degrees, whoever opens your link can transpose it.

7/ tests run against the real modules, not a copy. 4,032 chord combinations, 3,000 randomised codec round trips, and three assertions that music/ never touches the dom.

LinkedInThe process case study+ carousel 
I wrote a spec for a music app, then reviewed my own spec before building it. I found fourteen problems.

The worst one: the spec promised that you could share a link to the music you made. But the thing it actually saved was seven settings. Open a friend's link and you'd get a silent instrument tuned the same way as theirs. Not their music. The feature everyone was excited about did not exist in the data model at all.

That is not a coding bug. It is a specification that sounds complete when you read it and falls apart the moment you ask what exactly gets written to disk.

A few others, since the pattern is more useful than the list:

· One interaction was specified two contradictory ways in two different sections, and both were written by me, a week apart.
· The core gesture required two hands on a device I had called "first class mobile" three pages earlier.
· The feature I had named as the main differentiator shipped switched off by default, behind a toggle most people never find.

What I changed in how I work: before building, I now write out the exact object that gets saved. Not the feature list, the actual shape of the data. Almost every gap above was visible the moment I did that, and invisible for the eight pages before it.

The instrument is free and runs in a browser tab. Seven buttons, any key, no music theory needed.

LinkedInEngineering honestyweek 2 
My app went silent after about thirty seconds of recording and I could not work out why. The screen said a chord was playing. Nothing came out.

The cause was a voice limit I had written myself. I capped how many notes could sound at once, which is sensible. But I counted them at the moment they were handed to the audio clock, not while they were actually sounding. The app schedules a whole loop in advance, so four chords across two layers blew past the limit instantly and everything after it was silently dropped.

The limit was doing exactly what I told it to. I had told it the wrong thing.

Two things came out of this.

The fix was not a bigger number. It was counting only the notes whose sounding windows genuinely overlap, and when the ceiling is hit, cutting off the longest ringing note rather than refusing the newest. Silencing the chord someone just played is the worst option available, and the original code chose it every time.

The second thing mattered more. The app was one large file with functions reassigned at the end to hook behaviour defined earlier. That structure had already swallowed a change once, shipping a version where a control erased its own label. So I rewrote it into nineteen modules where nothing patches anything, and added tests that fail if that creeps back.

The bug was a bad line of code. The reason it was hard to find was the architecture around it.

Why this works. A specific debugging story with a real mechanism, and a conclusion that is not "test more". Engineers share this kind of post; it is the one most likely to travel beyond your own network.

Risks

Three things most likely to go wrong

iOS Safari audio is untested
The phone layout is built and verified: one screen, record pinned, transform ring under the thumb. What is still unknown is the audio on a real iPhone, specifically cold-start latency and the unlock gesture, because every test so far was Chrome with touch emulation. TikTok and Instagram will deliver most of your first traffic on exactly that device. Press a pad on your own phone before you post. It is the last unknown and it takes a minute.
The relay needs a first push
A challenge with no entries looks dead and stays dead. Get three friends to post entries in the first twenty four hours so the hashtag is never empty. This is normal and everyone does it.
Dev audiences and producer audiences do not mix
The X thread that lands with developers is noise to a beatmaker, and the opposite. Do not cross-post. Keep the accounts' voices separate even though they are the same person.
