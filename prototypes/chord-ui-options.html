Seven Degrees

Three working interaction models for the same instrument, each playable right here with a real chord engine and real audio. Pick with your hands, not your eyes — your PRD names "the controller feels bad" as risk #1, and that is not a risk you can read your way out of. Below the rigs: the UX bottlenecks I found in the spec.

Recommendation

Ship B's gesture on A's layout, and steal C's strip

The separate joystick is a hardware constraint, not a design decision. HiChord has a physical stick because a 7-button box has nowhere else to put it. A touchscreen has the finger already on the pad — a second control to reach for is a tax you are choosing to pay. Start the transform gesture from the pad itself, lay the pads out like A for familiarity, and add C's eight-slot strip so the share link plays something. That composite is roughly A's build cost plus three days.

UX review

Fourteen bottlenecks in the PRD

Ordered by what will hurt most. Section numbers refer to your spec.

Blocker

The share link doesn't share the music

§27 · §28 · §48 · §54

§54 ends "discover a progression → save → share", and §28 calls sharing the strongest distribution loop. But §27's payload is seven pad configs. There is no progression in the state model at all — nothing records that the user played 1, 4, 5, 6. A friend opens the link and gets a silent instrument tuned the same way, which is not what was promised and will not spread.

FixAdd an ordered slots: Array<{degree, transform} | null> of 8 and a bpm to the state model, plus a play button. That is not the looper you ruled out in §48 — no audio recording, no tracks, no overdub — and it is the difference between a link that plays and a link that doesn't.
Blocker

applyChordTransform(chord, transform) can't be implemented

§15 · §16 · §13

The signature takes notes and returns notes, but the transforms need context the notes no longer carry. "Add 7th" on pad 2 of C major must produce Dm7, on pad 5 G7, on pad 1 Cmaj7 — one transform, three qualities, decided by the scale. And "toggle the 3rd" has to know which note is the third, which an inverted or octave-shifted note array has lost. Build it as written and you get a layer that re-derives the chord by guessing at intervals.

FixTransforms operate on a spec, not on notes: {rootPc, intervals[], degree, scale} → {rootPc, intervals[]}. Voicing, inversion and octave are applied strictly after, in one place. Pipeline becomes degree → quality → transform → voicing → notes, and §16's "chord identity and voicing are separate" becomes true in the code rather than only in the doc.
Blocker

Momentary or sticky? The spec says both

§14 · §27 · §37 · §41

§14 says the controller "transforms the currently played chord" — momentary. §27 and §41 store transform on each pad and in the preset — sticky. §41 also keeps a global transform at the root of the state, so the same value lives in two places with no stated precedence. Every downstream question (what does a preset restore, what does the puck show when you let go, does pad 5 stay a 7th chord) depends on an answer that isn't in the document.

FixPick momentary-with-latch: the controller is live while held, and releasing writes the last transform onto that pad. Delete the root-level transform; the held pad's value is the only truth. Both prototypes here latch, and it is the behaviour that makes presets coherent.
High

The core interaction needs two hands on the device it was promised for

§25 · §36 · §37

§25 makes mobile first-class and §37 makes "hold a pad while moving the controller" the defining interaction. On a phone those are in direct conflict: holding a pad with one thumb and dragging a separate puck with the other means no hand is holding the phone. §36's layout makes it worse by putting the controller at top-centre, outside the thumb zone entirely — about three quarters of phone use is one-handed.

FixStart the transform gesture from the pad (Option B). If you keep a separate controller, it belongs in the bottom third, pads mirrored opposite, and the whole rig moves below the midpoint of the screen.
High

Phase 6 needs a clock that Phase 0 never builds

§49 · §34 · §52

Phases 0–5 contain no transport, no tempo, no scheduler — everything is triggered by touch. Phase 6 then asks for a 12-pulse timeline with per-voice micro-timing offsets. Micro-timing means sample-accurate scheduling against AudioContext.currentTime, which is an architectural property of how every voice is started, not a module you add later. §52 says adding a feature shouldn't require rewriting unrelated components; this one will.

FixPut a transport in Phase 0 — a lookahead scheduler and a when parameter on every note-on, even though MVP always passes "now". It's maybe 80 lines, and it is what makes the progression strip, the metronome and the entire rhythm engine additive instead of a rebuild.
High

The headline feature ships switched off

§11.2 · §45 Risk 2 · §54

Per-pad sound is called a core MVP feature, and the mitigation for it being overwhelming is to default every pad to the same sound with Link Sounds on. Most users never change a default. The thing that makes the product different from every other chord tool is therefore invisible on first run, and the §54 north star — where changing pad 4 to a warm pad is the moment of discovery — never happens.

FixShip Link Sounds off with a deliberately varied default kit (keys, pad, pluck, keys, synth, keys, bass) so the difference is audible within the first four taps, and put Link Sounds one tap away for anyone who wants uniformity. Also specify what the toggle does to existing assignments: linking must preserve each pad's value and override at playback, never overwrite it, or every user who toggles it loses their work.
High

Audio unlocks on the gesture it's supposed to respond to

§44 · §45 Risk 4 · §22

"Audio context initialized on first valid gesture" means the first gesture is the first chord — so the first chord is late, quiet, or silent while the context spins up, and on iOS Safari a cold context plus sample loading can run into hundreds of milliseconds. First impression of an instrument is first-note latency. Sample-backed sounds (§22's talking drum, shekere) make it worse and the spec never mentions a loading state.

FixUnlock on the first pointerdown anywhere on the document, including the key and scale selectors, and warm one silent voice through the graph at unlock. Set latencyHint:'interactive' and drop Tone.js's default 100ms lookahead for direct triggers. Gate any sampled sound behind a visible loading state and never let a pad be tappable before its buffer is decoded.
Medium

Presets promise a progression the state model can't hold

§26 · §41

§26 says a preset configures "progression, pad sounds, transformations, voicings" and lists I–V–vi–IV. InstrumentState in §41 has no progression field, so as specced a preset can only set seven pads' sounds — the chord progression part of a chord preset is unrepresentable. Same root cause as the share gap, and both disappear with the same fix.

FixLand the slots array first; presets and sharing then become serialisations of one object instead of two features that each need their own invented format.
Medium

The transform vocabulary mixes relative and absolute operations

§14 · §15

The union is "base" | "minor" | "maj7" | "min7" | "dom7" | "sus4" | "augmented", but the table that fills it says Up = "toggle 3rd: major ↔ minor". A toggle is relative to whatever the degree already is; "minor" is absolute. Store a toggle as "minor" and the user's up-gesture on pad 2 (already minor) either does nothing or silently means something else. Same for Right = "add 7th", which resolves to three different absolute values depending on degree.

FixMake every transform relative and diatonic: flip3, add7, sus4, sus2, add9, six, dom7, aug. add7 reads the seventh out of the scale, so one transform produces maj7, m7 and m7♭5 correctly and keeps working when the user switches scale. The rigs above do exactly this — press 7 in C major and add the 7th, and you get Bm7♭5 without a special case.
Medium

"Toggle the 3rd" produces an unnameable chord on degree 7

§14 · §51

I swept all 3,024 combinations of 12 keys × 4 scales × 7 degrees × 9 transforms through the engine in these prototypes. Everything resolves to a real chord except one case: flipping the third of a diminished triad. On vii° in major that gives root + major 3rd + flat 5th — a chord with no name and no use, and the spec's Up gesture lands on it every time a user holds pad 7. The same hole appears on ii° in natural minor, vi in Dorian and iii in Mixolydian.

FixMake the toggle resolve the fifth as well: diminished becomes minor, augmented becomes major. Then pad 7 Up gives Bm, which is the substitution a player would reach for anyway. Add the exhaustive sweep to §51's test list — "every key × scale × degree × transform yields a named chord" is one assertion and it caught this in a second.
Medium

Keyboard controls collide with the browser

§24 · §53

Arrow keys scroll the page, and Space activates whatever button has focus — so the moment a user tabs to a pad and presses Space to "hold", they retrigger the focused control instead. Both bindings fight the platform rather than using it, and the acceptance criteria only check that keyboard "works".

FixpreventDefault on the instrument's own key set while the rig has focus, make Space hold the last played pad rather than a focused one, and give pads a real focus ring with aria-pressed. Add prefers-reduced-motion to §53 while you're there — it isn't mentioned anywhere.
Medium

Seven simultaneous instruments is a mobile voice budget problem

§19 · §21 · §23

Seven pads with independent sounds, polyphonic chords of three to four notes, plus global reverb and delay. Instantiate each pad's synth eagerly and you have ~28 voices and seven effect sends live on a phone. §21 correctly says don't create a synth per press but doesn't say what to create instead, and §23's global reverb on sustained pad sounds will turn a four-chord progression into mud within two bars.

FixOne pooled voice allocator with a hard cap (16 is plenty) and oldest-voice stealing; instantiate only the distinct sounds actually assigned, not one per pad. Put the reverb send per sound rather than globally — a bass and a pluck want almost none of it.
Medium

Chords all hit at once, so the instrument sounds static

§22 · §39 · §52

Nothing in the spec articulates notes in time. Every chord is a simultaneous block, which is the one thing a real player never does — and §52's quality bar is "sounds intentionally musical rather than like a Web Audio demo". A block triad on a soft pad is precisely what a Web Audio demo sounds like.

FixOne per-sound parameter: strum spread in milliseconds, 0 for keys, 18–35ms for plucks and strings. It costs a loop index times a constant in the note-on call and buys more perceived quality than any additional preset. It also depends on the Phase 0 transport, which is a third reason to build that first.
Medium

Dorian and Mixolydian buy almost nothing in MVP

§9 · §45 Risk 5

Against major, Mixolydian changes the quality of two of seven pads and Dorian three. Without a progression playing, a user switching scale hears a difference only if they happen to press one of the changed degrees — so the feature is real work for a payoff most users won't perceive. Meanwhile the two items that would be felt immediately (the progression strip, strum) are out of scope.

FixShip Major and Natural Minor for MVP. The architecture is data-driven either way, so the other two are a one-line addition later — spend the time on something audible. If you keep all four, at least put the scale change where a loop is running so it can be heard.
One more thing

Your originality clause points at the wrong layer

§4.5 asks not to clone HiChord's interface, but §14's transform table is close to a transcription of theirs: up flips major/minor, up-right dominant 7th, down sus4, up-left augmented. The visual language is the part you were planning to change, and the directional mapping is the part you were planning to keep — that is backwards relative to what the spec says you're worried about. I'm not the person to tell you where the legal line sits, and it's worth an actual opinion from someone who is. As product advice: the mapping is a data table, so make it one, and let the gesture topology be yours. Option B's transform ring doesn't exist on a device with a physical joystick, which is the kind of difference that reads as a different instrument rather than a different skin.
