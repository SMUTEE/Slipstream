Knob on a keyboard

Twelve angles, no diagonals to guess at

Arrow keys only reach four of the knob's twelve detents. The fix is a nine-key cluster that sits under your left hand in the same spatial arrangement as the knob itself, plus two step keys that reach the four positions between the compass points.

1 – 7

Play a scale degree. Right hand on the number row, left hand on the cluster.

Q W E / A S D / Z X C

The eight compass detents. S returns the knob to the plain triad.

[  ]

Step the knob one detent counter-clockwise or clockwise — this is how you reach all twelve.

↑ → ↓ ←

The four cardinals. Hold two at once for a diagonal, the way a real stick works.

Space

Latch the chord so your hands are free to change key and hear it slide.

G

Toggle slide. Esc recentres the knob.

V

Cycle the voicing — root position, first inversion, second inversion. O shifts the octave.

R

Record. Starts the loop if it isn't running, and commits the layer when you press it again.

Enter

Start or stop the loop. Backspace deletes the last layer.

Four detents — 6th, 9th, 7sus4, dim — sit between the compass points and are reachable only by stepping or dragging. That is deliberate: the eight that earn a dedicated key are the eight you reach for while playing, and the ordering puts each step-only detent next to its nearest relative, so stepping one click from a key you know always lands somewhere musical.
